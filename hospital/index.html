🏥 PAMO Hospital Prototype

What if we built a hospital from scratch in 2026 — with PAMO as the operating system?

The Premise

Every existing hospital is a legacy system. Layers of bureaucracy accumulated over decades. Roles confused. Incentives misaligned. Makers buried under administrative control. Principals absent or captured.

But what if you started fresh? What if you designed a hospital the way you'd design a company in 2026 — AI-native, structurally immune to bureaucratic decay, with PAMO principles baked into the architecture from day one?

Design Principles

  • Skin in the Game: Every decision-maker bears consequence proportional to their authority
  • Separation Principle: Clinical innovation and clinical production live in separate structures
  • Four Roles Clarity: Every person knows whether they are P, A, M, or OSA at any given moment
  • AI Enforcement Layer: Real-time audit, memory layer, structural drift detection — without surveillance
  • Maker Protection: Rare-skill clinicians have structural immunity from bureaucratic override

Inspiration

This prototype is inspired by institutions that dared to start fresh — like Dell Medical Center (UT Austin, opened 2016), which was designed without legacy departments and with value-based care as a first principle. PAMO takes that ambition further: not just redesigning care delivery, but redesigning the organizational immune system itself.

Vision

"The best hospital in 2030 won't be the one with the most technology. It will be the one with the least bureaucracy."
— PAMO Hospital design premise
"If you can't fire the bureaucrat who blocks the surgeon — you don't have a hospital, you have a government office with operating rooms."
— Design principle #1
